✅ Release 2.1.0.25: the post-quantum era

This is described as a substantial release that changed how development is done and also changed the architecture and security assumptions of the protocol. Consensus, peer-to-peer communication, onion routing, proofs, node storage and confidential tokens were all reworked, and the whole toolchain was rebuilt from the ground up in Rust.

Consensus: the customized HotStuff BFT algorithm used up to 2.1.0.24 caused many headaches. It was replaced with Commonware, an open and heavily battle-tested implementation, after a small modification to support the Falcon post-quantum signature scheme. The same pragmatic approach was applied elsewhere: wherever an off-the-shelf, proven component could replace a custom one, it was swapped in.

Frames and epochs: the network now works with frames at the global level and at the app shard level, with a target of roughly 10 seconds per frame. That makes 360 frames an hour and an epoch of 720 frames, about two hours. The predictable rhythm helps debugging and gives node operators a clearer idea of when shards appear, split or merge.

Networking and privacy: the pub/sub mesh and gRPC streaming layers remain, but are now built on standard parts with post-quantum additions. According to Cassandra, Quilibrium is now the first post-quantum onion routing network, while most Tor traffic still uses non-post-quantum cryptography.

Storage and recovery: KZG and Verkle trees were replaced by a hash-based Merkle forest (the Jellyfish Merkle forest), which gives smaller proofs and simpler synchronization. Archive nodes still hold the full network data, and authenticated checkpoints plus replay-based restoration make it much easier for nodes to catch up.

Provers: a new data availability proof model, similar in spirit to Filecoin's WindowPoSt, uses STARKs instead of SNARKs. It also gives provers more flexibility in getting proofs to the network, so interruptions in a mesh network do not unfairly cost them rewards.

For operators and developers: everything, including QClient, is now in Rust, which makes building simpler. There are new reachability checks, detailed diagnostics for shard and prover status, and opt-in peer diagnostics, since anything potentially sensitive stays opt-in. The Explorer is now shard-aware. Wallet developers get better transaction history tooling, simpler and more targeted state sync, and lattice-based confidential token proofs, which matters for key management.

✅ Mainnet status

Cassandra addresses the confusion and the criticism from detractors: mainnet has been live for a while. Stability under 2.1.0.25 was reached in late October. All primitives are unlocked, and shards split and merge without interruption. New things are deployed to the network all the time.

At the time of the stream there were 322 dedicated provers, a number that had roughly doubled in a few days and is expected to keep growing. Frame time is currently about 16 seconds, slower than the 10-second target, and work continues to improve it. Throughput grows as more provers are dedicated to more shards.

✅ Industry news: the Binance hack

Cassandra discusses the Binance hack from the end of the previous month, in which about 6.3 billion dollars in crypto assets were reportedly taken, with an initial investigation pointing to a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop. In her view, a single compromised laptop should never be enough for such a loss. She published an article on Twitter explaining how hot wallet operations should be done, with a diagram showing how to compartmentalize people, DevOps and operations. It draws on her experience on the core cryptography team at Coinbase.

This connects to QKMS, the Quilibrium Key Management Service. Unlike a traditional hardware security module, QKMS uses multi-party computation, so keys are split into shards across several parties. A user can hold one shard on their own device while another lives on the Quilibrium mainnet, and the wallet provider holds none. This brings the security profile of large exchanges into the hands of regular users. The transaction service and chain indexer service from the same diagram will be delivered as MegaRPC.

✅ Industry news: Farcaster and Hypersnap

Farcaster was acquired by Balaji's Network School, which carried out a mass purge of users it considered undesirable. Cassandra notes that many of the "offenses" in the exposed list were simply arguing with the previous team. In response, Hypersnap, launched earlier, became a full hard fork, so the broader Farcaster network now lives on Hypersnap while the Network School app is left on its own limited chain.

In one month, community members coordinated to inform users about the alternatives, including a protect-the-protocol protest at DevCon during Vitalik's keynote. According to Cassandra, over half of the Farcaster network has since moved to Quorum and other apps. The episode fits Quilibrium's stance as an advocate for freedom of speech and freedom of cryptography, and for self-sovereignty over data.

On frame times faster than 10 seconds: the VDF now exists only at the global level, since the data availability proofs already take long enough at the application level. The 10-second target is part of the logic that scales difficulty, which for the VDF means the number of squaring steps.

✅ Roadmap: 2.1.0.26

The 2.1.0.26 sub-patch focuses on frame time and on node operators who hit edge cases. The main goals are lower bandwidth use and lower memory use, especially on Macs, where macOS memory management hits a particular edge case with the memory-heavy processes introduced in 2.1.0.25. The general hardware recommendation is one core, two gigabytes of RAM and four gigabytes of storage. A recursive STARK is also in the works to compress the validation work provers handle, so the frame rate can go back to 10 seconds or faster.

✅ Roadmap: MegaRPC and Coppice

MegaRPC will launch as a public service for wallets and app developers. Today most apps rely on providers like Alchemy or Infura, which log requests, exposing users' wallet addresses and IP addresses. MegaRPC instead uses oblivious RAM lookups and does the indexing and data formatting itself, so requests stay private to the requester. An SDK shim sits in front of the standard libraries developers already use and translates standard RPC calls into oblivious RAM queries. Launch support covers Quilibrium mainnet, standard EVM chains, Bitcoin, Solana, Bittensor and Kaspa.

Coppice, coming shortly, replaces the end-to-end encrypted Channels SDK. It offers a much simpler interface for messenger-style apps and true post-quantum cryptography in place of the elliptic curve based triple ratchet. It also solves history synchronization and catch-up directly in the implementation, rather than leaving developers to build it at the application layer. It is coming to Quorum and other Farcaster clients first, and is generic enough for anyone to use.

✅ The bidirectional bridge and post-quantum safety

The new bidirectional bridge is not limited to QUIL and wrapped QUIL. It can bring in any ERC-20 token and ETH itself. Because all tokens on Quilibrium are private, this enables something that barely exists in crypto: a dark pool, a trading venue where large trades happen privately without moving the public market.

Cassandra also describes the bridge as a possible safe harbor. If quantum computers, or a mathematical breakthrough against elliptic curve cryptography, broke other chains, assets could be moved into a post-quantum state, because the bridge proof is STARK-based and does not rely on elliptic curve assumptions. She argues that seed-phrase-based recovery proposals would not help holders whose addresses come from raw private keys, and that some teams overstate how post-quantum they really are.

✅ Roadmap: QNS, Klearu and MetaVM

QNS, the Quilibrium Name Service, will get quality-of-life improvements and a post-quantum upgrade, and depends on MegaRPC.

Klearu provides private AI through multi-party computation, for both training and inference. Support was recently added for several image models, and the team is training its own transformer-based image model that fits the MPC execution model, can run across the network on regular CPUs, and aims at quality similar to leading commercial image generators. Cassandra stresses that this is provably private, unlike TEE-based approaches that amount to trusting the hardware, and that it suits applications that normally depend on a third-party AI service.

MetaVM is the zero-knowledge and MPC-based virtualization library, targeting RISC-V, EVM and the Solana runtime. It provides general confidential and verifiable compute, and the RISC-V side has been very stable, even running multi-party Doom as a demo. Klearu and MetaVM are both targeted for the 2.1.1 release.

✅ Roadmap: QConsole and Quorum Mobile

MetaVM will be required for FFX, an upcoming QWeb services release, and Relational will arrive alongside it, offering RDS and Keyspaces style shims over the hypergraph with private data and private queries. MegaRPC will be embedded in QConsole, and QClient integration will let users launch tokens or put raw data on the network directly from QConsole without opening a terminal. This will be the topic of the next stream.

Quorum Mobile has been reworked to be fully native, in Kotlin for Android and Swift for iOS, replacing React Native while keeping the same look. It will include free Farcaster account onboarding, deeper Quilibrium integration and support for Snap Compute, a small virtual machine inside the feed. Cassandra sees this as the real test of Farcaster's original ambition of a Turing-complete social network, where people can share, play and remix interactive applications. Hypersnap has already gone through many releases, with 16 people on the organization.
